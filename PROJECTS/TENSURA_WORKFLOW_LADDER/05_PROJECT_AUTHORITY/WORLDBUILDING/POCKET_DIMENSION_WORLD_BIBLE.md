## 2. Core concept

**USER-DIRECTED:** Gobta's pocket dimension is a genuine, explorable world rather than a storage menu or empty construction room. It has terrain, structures, resources, living space, hostile environments, creatures and room for long-term farms, workshops and mechanisms.

The first world Gobta enters is generated from an extraordinarily fortunate and unusual seed. Its starting region places three strategically valuable Minecraft structures within a connected, explorable area:

1. An exceptionally enormous, surface-exposed **Ancient City**.
2. A very large **village**.
3. A **Trial Chamber** within practical travelling distance of the village.

The arrangement is a rare starting-world generation anomaly. It should feel like a remarkable seed discovery, not like three structures were artificially placed next to one another for convenience.

## 3. Starting-region generation and geography

### 3.1 The unusual seed

- **USER-DIRECTED:** Gobta's first pocket-world spawn uses a uniquely fortunate seed.
- **USER-DIRECTED:** The Ancient City is exposed at the surface and rises to, or occupies terrain at, the world's normal upper build-limit region rather than being buried deep underground.
- **USER-DIRECTED:** The Ancient City is exceptionally large, far beyond the ordinary impression of a standard Ancient City.
- **OPEN:** Exact seed string, world version, world border, build-height number, coordinates, biome layout and generation explanation.
- Do not invent a numeric seed or coordinates. The world-generation anomaly is an AU rule until a concrete seed is selected and tested.
- If a real Java seed is later chosen, verify it against the selected game version and record whether the AU requires modified generation.

### 3.2 Landmark A: The enormous exposed Ancient City

**USER-DIRECTED**
- This is the Minecraft structure called an **Ancient City**. “Skull city” was an informal description, not its official structure name.
- It is enormous and visually dominates part of the starting landscape.
- It is exposed to open air at or near the upper world-limit region instead of being hidden deep below the surface.
- Its sculk, ruined architecture, bridges, corridors, towers, platforms and deep-city atmosphere should make it feel ancient, imposing and dangerous.
- It is a major landmark and a long-term exploration destination, not an early free loot chest.

**OPEN**
- Exact footprint, number of districts, vertical arrangement, entrances, condition, sculk spread, Warden behavior, loot distribution and whether the structure is one connected city or a generation-linked complex.
- Exact distance and direction from spawn, village and Trial Chamber.
- Whether the exposed position creates environmental hazards such as extreme drops, exposed bridges, weather or difficult access.

### 3.3 Landmark B: The large village

**USER-DIRECTED**
- A substantial village exists within the broader starting region.
- It should feel like a genuinely large settlement, not a tiny cluster of a few houses.
- It is close enough to become a practical early development location, but the three landmarks must retain believable geography.
- Its likely long-term uses include food, crops, livestock, villagers, trading, building materials, population and future automated farms.

**OPEN**
- Village biome, exact size, number of buildings, population, professions, golems, farms, surrounding terrain, defensive condition and exact distance from the spawn/Ancient City/Trial Chamber.
- Whether Gobta encounters it inhabited and functioning, partially damaged, threatened or in another state.

### 3.4 Landmark C: The Trial Chamber

**USER-DIRECTED**
- A Trial Chamber is located within travelling distance of the village.
- It is not directly attached to the village or placed immediately beside its central buildings.
- It is close enough to become a meaningful early-to-mid-term exploration target.
- It offers a future combat challenge and potential source of useful loot and materials, subject to the selected world version and the story's progression.

**OPEN**
- Exact location, distance, entrance, chamber size, trial spawner layout, vault access, ominous-trial availability, loot and threat level.
- Whether Gobta discovers it during Chapter One or only records/locates it for a later expedition.

### 3.5 Relative map and route logic

The starting region must have a coherent spatial layout. Current relationship constraints:

- Spawn is positioned so Gobta can discover and orient himself in the unusual region.
- The Ancient City is a huge, surface-visible landmark at the upper build-limit terrain.
- The village is a substantial settlement within the same regional map.
- The Trial Chamber is near enough to the village for planned expeditions, but not immediately beside it.
- The structures must not be placed on top of one another or made to look like one composite structure.
- Exact compass directions, coordinates, travel times, terrain barriers and sightlines remain **OPEN** until the map is designed.

## 5. Strategic value of the starting region

### 5.1 Village development
Potential uses after Gobta has assessed the settlement:
- Reliable food production and crop expansion.
- Livestock and animal-resource systems.
- Villager trading and access to useful professions.
- A possible early base, workshop or logistics hub.
- Future automated farms and storage.
- A population/settlement that can become a meaningful part of the pocket world's development.

These are development opportunities, not automatic benefits. Gobta must earn trust, gather materials, build infrastructure and solve practical problems.

### 5.2 Ancient City exploration
Potential uses:
- High-risk exploration and discovery.
- Sculk-related research and environmental experimentation.
- Valuable loot and rare materials if supported by the chosen version.
- A large architectural site that can later be mapped, secured or repurposed.
- A major hazard that rewards preparation, stealth, equipment and planning.

Do not let Gobta clear or exploit the entire city immediately. Its scale and danger should exceed his opening resources.

### 5.3 Trial Chamber expeditions
Potential uses:
- Combat practice and tactical testing.
- Trial spawners and vault-related rewards, if the chosen version supports them.
- A source of materials and loot for later engineering.
- A controlled progression milestone after Gobta has improved his equipment and preparation.

The Trial Chamber should not be treated as a free early-game loot dispenser.

## 6. Chapter One discovery boundary

**USER-DIRECTED:** Gobta first enters this pocket dimension during the long opening Chapter One, before the story reconnects to the Tensura source-event sequence.

Chapter One may establish:
- The first sensory impression of the world and the scale of its terrain.
- Gobta's discovery of the unusual seed and the three landmarks.
- Initial exploration and a first rough regional mental map.
- Tests of block interaction, inventory, crafting and the world's physical rules.
- Initial resource gathering and survival decisions.
- The first enchantment exploit and construction of The Yeeter, as detailed in the master character sheet.
- Early recognition that XP is a scarce progression resource.
- A realistic first plan for food, safety, resource collection and later farms.

Chapter One does **not** need to follow the source-volume chapter/scene order while it remains inside the pocket-dimension opening. It must still establish a causal bridge to Chapter Two, where the story begins moving toward the source-connected Tensura events. The exact point of exit, transition and first source anchor belongs in the arc plan and must later be reconciled against verified Volume 1 source material.

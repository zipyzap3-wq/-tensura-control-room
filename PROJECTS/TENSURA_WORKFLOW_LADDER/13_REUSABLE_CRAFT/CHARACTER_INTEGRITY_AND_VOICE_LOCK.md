# Character Integrity and Voice Lock
## Non-negotiable rule
A scene is not character-correct merely because its events match the source or its dialogue sounds plausible. Each character must behave like the specific person established by source evidence and approved project chapters. Plot compliance never excuses personality drift. If a plot beat and established characterization appear to conflict, design a character-consistent route or ask the user when a major divergence is required.

## Required reading
Before drafting or revising:
1. Read the complete previous approved chapter and relevant prior scenes.
2. Read dossiers/time-state cards for characters who speak, act, are observed or materially affect the scene.
3. Read source chunks and current continuity/knowledge/relationship ledgers.
4. Reconcile summaries against actual prose and source.
5. If required material is missing, report the exact gap and stop the affected work.

## Voice card
For each major or recurring character record:
- speech rhythm, vocabulary, formality and directness;
- humor style and tolerance for silence;
- emotional tells and stress behavior;
- default tactics in disagreement, danger and affection;
- values, contradictions, blind spots and self-deception;
- relationship-specific behavior;
- what they would never casually say or do;
- source anchors and confidence.

## Character independence
Supporting characters have their own objectives, agency, competence and limits. Do not make them foolish to make another character impressive. Group scenes must preserve distinct reactions; characters do not all notice, understand or respond to the same thing identically.

## Decisions and emotional logic
Decisions must arise from motives, knowledge, pressure, personality and available options, not merely from plot convenience. Reactions need a stimulus. Track how embarrassment, fear, affection, anger, loyalty, status and self-interest change behavior. Realistic reactions should not flatten a character's competence or force timid behavior without evidence.

## Pre-draft test
For each central character ask:
- What do they want now?
- What do they know and misunderstand?
- What would they notice first?
- What tactic would they choose?
- What would they conceal?
- How would this specific relationship change the reaction?
- What behavior would be out of character?

## Post-draft audit
Flag generic lines, voice swaps, unearned reactions, inconsistent motives, flattened supporting characters, knowledge leaks and incorrectly attributed actions. If a central character fails the voice audit, the chapter is not ready for presentation. Correct the draft and record the evidence.

# Light-Novel and Serial Narrative Method
## Governing distinction
Planning decides what happens. Craft decides how clearly, vividly and effectively it is told. Craft must not silently change the author's intended story.

## Narrative target
- Immediate, character-led, readable prose suitable for serial reading.
- Close, purposeful viewpoint filtered through the active character's attention, knowledge, priorities and emotional state.
- Dialogue with a job: persuade, conceal, test, threaten, comfort, bargain, evade, connect or reveal.
- Clear, vivid, economical description. Concrete details should orient, reveal character, shape mood or clarify action.
- Humor and emotion emerge from character and context, not generic punchlines.
- Vary action, conversation, investigation, planning, intimacy, aftermath, humor and reflection.

## Scene and sequel
A scene generally moves through goal → opposition → tactics → escalation → outcome/turn → consequence. A sequel may move through reaction → dilemma → decision. This is a diagnostic model, not a rigid formula. Every scene must change something or deliberately establish pressure, information or relationship movement.

## Causality and transitions
Events should follow from decisions, constraints and prior consequences. Avoid scenes that happen only because the outline says so. Transitions should identify where characters are, why they move, what time has passed and what connects the next scene. Name locations and orient the reader; do not use vague, untracked travel or unnamed transitions.

## Viewpoint
Choose narrative person, viewpoint character, distance and information permissions before drafting. A POV switch must earn its place and be unmistakable. Never reveal another character's private thoughts through accidental drift.

## Action readability
Track bodies, distance, terrain, obstacles, sightlines, objects, timing, injuries and resource costs. Make cause/effect legible. Action is not a blur of dramatic verbs.

## Chapter movement
A chapter needs meaningful movement, not an arbitrary word quota. Manage tension and breathing room. Endings may resolve, reveal, turn emotionally, raise danger or create a question; do not force a cliffhanger. Openings should reconnect to prior consequence and establish the immediate situation.

## Review passes
1. Event order and causality.
2. Character motives, voice and knowledge.
3. Clarity, staging and physical logic.
4. Dialogue and subtext.
5. Pace, emotional consequence and scene purpose.
6. Line edit for vagueness, repetition and clutter.
7. Serial handoff and unresolved promises.
